Weekly recommendations · 03 Aug 2026

Home, then home alone.

A week of two halves. Wednesday's a write-off in the best way — the Dallas flight lands in Manchester at 7pm, then straight onto a train to Chorley, so Wednesday evening wants nothing more strenuous than a sofa. Thursday's a vet trip for the animals, ordinary as Tuesdays go. Then Friday evening Holly's off to Hannah's hen do, which leaves you the weekend to yourself — so half this list is built for the jet lag, the other half for having the house, the dog and the decisions all to yourself.

Music

Geese

Brooklyn's loudest current export — avant-garde post-punk with actual hooks buried in the noise, which is rarer than it sounds. Not background music exactly, but decent company for unpacking a suitcase you'd rather not be dealing with.

Reading

The Two of Us at the End of the World — C. A. Fletcher

A standalone about how the world quietly emptied of people, told through two characters working out how to survive what's left — mystery and otherness over spectacle, and crucially finished in one book. A good change of genre after a run of magic systems, without asking for a six-volume commitment.

Screen

Ted Lasso, Season 4

Premieres 5 August — the exact evening you land, as it happens, which feels like scheduling rather than coincidence. The newly formed women's team gives it a genuinely fresh shape rather than just more of the same, but it's still the same breezy mix of optimism and hard-won charm underneath. Correct viewing for a jet-lagged sofa.

Comedy

Garth Marenghi (Matthew Holness), live

Matthew Holness back in character as horror novelist and self-declared "dream-weaver" Garth Marenghi, reading from his latest masterpiece with total, unearned confidence. Deeply silly in the specific, layered way that rewards already knowing the bit — which, given the Darkplace devotion, you do.

Podcast

Football Ramble

Already on the regular rotation, but worth flagging now the season's properly back — good company for the solo weekend, and better company than the actual football news most weeks.

Hobby · Gaming

Crimson Desert

A properly big single-player fantasy RPG — dynamic combat, a sprawling continent, and the kind of deep storytelling that rewards actually having a free weekend rather than stealing an hour here and there. With the house to yourself and no consultation required on what's on the telly, this is the moment.

Food & drink Chorley area

The Cherry Tree at Blackrod

Independently owned, unpretentious by design, seasonal produce sourced as locally as the menu can manage. Nothing showy, which is precisely the point — a solid Saturday night out, solo or otherwise.

Days out Chorley area

Rivington Pike walk + Rivington Brewery Co tap

A proper loop around reservoirs, forest and moorland, finishing at the brewery's own taproom for a beer brewed about fifty feet from where you're drinking it, plus food from the stalls parked outside. Bring Morris — he's earned it, and so, frankly, have you after Wednesday.
