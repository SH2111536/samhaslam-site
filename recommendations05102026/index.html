Weekly recommendations · 05 Oct 2026

Over to the Riviera.

Tuesday evening, a flight out of Heathrow trades Chorley for Cannes — first time this list has had to think in euros. Holly's holding the fort solo (Sandbach Market, Friday), and you're back in time for Bolton v Stoke on Saturday. Mostly a Cannes-shaped list this week, with a little something either side for the travelling and the match.

Music

Slow Fiction — "dollhouse"

A New York four-piece working the same seam as Yo La Tengo and Broken Social Scene — hooky but unhurried, built for a long train-then-plane day rather than a sprint.

Reading

The Scarlet Ball — Nghi Vo

Gilded Age ballrooms with a proper Gothic undercurrent — bloodshed dressed up in finery, otherness built right into the premise. Standalone, and a decent match for a few hours over the Channel.

Screen

Slow Horses, Season 6

Gary Oldman back as Jackson Lamb, same deadpan rot at the centre of British intelligence, more puzzles and escalating pressure. Already a favourite — this is just notice that it's back, hotel-room viewing fully justified.

Podcast

The Secret World of Roald Dahl

Dramatic, thoughtful, and considerably stranger than the children's books let on — good company for the Heathrow wait, and a decent change of subject from work.

Food & drink Cannes

Aux Bon Enfants

Family-run for three generations, the opposite of Cannes chic, ingredients straight from the market next door — unfussy Provençal cooking exactly in line with the kind of place you two actually seek out abroad.

Food & drink Cannes

Astoux et Brun

A proper seafood institution — oysters and shellfish, walk-ins only, service running straight through from lunch to dinner. Worth the queue, by all accounts.

Days out Cannes

Île Saint-Honorat

A car-free island a short ferry ride out, built around a working fortified monastery — quiet, slow, properly removed from the Croisette circus. A circular walk of about 90 minutes, which is roughly the dose of solitude a work trip usually needs.

Food & drink Bolton, matchday

The Man & Scythe

One of the oldest pubs in Britain, dating to 1251 — if "characterful" is the brief, this clears the bar before you've even ordered. Proper real ales, proper history, proper pre-match pint.
