Weekly recommendations · 10 Aug 2026

Championship, opening day.

A quiet week with one enormous exception. Dentist Wednesday, Morris off to the groomers Thursday, and then Saturday: Bolton's first Championship match since 2018/19, kicking off at home to Preston North End. Everything else this week is either padding around that or recovery from it.

Music

Ratboys — "Singin' to an Empty Chair"

Guitar-based indie rock with just the right amount of twang, and the sort of hooky, heartfelt sing-alongs that reward being played too loud. Solid dentist-waiting-room antidote, if such a thing can be said to exist.

Reading

Daggerbound — T. Kingfisher

Kingfisher's whole appeal is inventive magic with a straight face and a sharp sense of humour running underneath it — closer in spirit to Sanderson's rigour than most "funny fantasy" manages. Standalone-friendly, no series debt incurred.

Screen

Sterling Point

A coming-of-age drama about a teenager who inherits her mysterious grandfather's island off the coast of Canada — mystery and inheritance doing a lot of the plot's heavy lifting, which tends to make for decent evening viewing when you don't want to think too hard between now and Saturday.

Comedy

Fern Brady — Manchester Castlefield Bowl

2 September, already on the "rates highly" list, and for once actually nearby rather than requiring a trip south. Worth booking now rather than filing under "someday."

Podcast

The Fanzone Pod

Bolton Wanderers, by the fans, for the fans — timely given Saturday is the club's first Championship match in seven years. Worth a listen on the way to the ground with your dad, purely for the pre-match nerves management.

Food & drink Bolton (matchday)

The Beehive, Lostock

Fifteen minutes' walk from the ground, three-meat carvery or the pub-classics route if you'd rather not commit before kick-off. Park there for a fiver and put it towards the bar tab — sensible, if not exactly in keeping with the spreadsheet-conscious instinct.

Food & drink Chorley area

The Shed, Chorley

Straightforward British cooking, no theatrics, which after a week bookended by a dentist appointment and a full-contact football match is roughly the correct register for an evening out.

Days out Chorley area

Yarrow Valley Country Park

Over 700 acres following the River Yarrow, just south of town — a properly gentle loop for a Sunday when Saturday's exertions (emotional or otherwise, depending how the match goes) have taken their toll. Morris will have opinions either way.
