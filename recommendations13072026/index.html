Weekly recommendations · 13 Jul 2026

Eight things for a settled week.

A quiet one on paper — home in Wheelton all week, work from the kitchen table, one evening out to watch England take on Argentina, and a birthday party at the weekend. No frantic dashing about required, so this week's list leans into that: things to put on, read, drink, and drive twenty minutes to, rather than anything requiring a passport.

Music

Dutch Interior — "It's Glass" EP

Southern Californian indie outfit, previously content with scrappy guitar rock, now apparently in possession of a Hammond organ and a grand piano and determined to use both. "It's Glass" is their turn towards something more spacious — still guitar-forward, still capable of a proper hook, just with room to breathe in the arrangement. If Wet Leg's deadpan snap and South Arcade's melodic instinct are your usual register, this sits comfortably alongside them without just repeating the trick. Good dog-walk listening; good kitchen-singalong listening too, once you've learned the words.

Reading

The Tricky Business of Faerie Bargains — Reena McCarty

A debut about a woman who spent over a century as a cook in the Wild King's castle after being taken as a child, now working a very mortal desk job finding loopholes in fairy contracts for a living — magical bureaucracy, essentially, played for wit rather than grimness. It has the "otherness" a good fantasy needs, a fair bit of charm, and crucially isn't part six of anything, so no waiting for the series to finish first. A palate-cleanser between the heavier Sanderson-scale reads, not a replacement for them.

Screen

Star City

The For All Mankind universe gets a sister series, telling the same alternate-history space race from the Soviet side — Rhys Ifans and Anna Maxwell Martin doing the kind of terse, committed work that makes political drama land. Dour in places, as you'd expect from a show about life under totalitarianism with occasional rocket launches, but it's the same blend of human drama and plausible engineering that made the original worth the time. Slots neatly next to Slow Horses and Silo in the "prestige drama, minimal nonsense" pile.

Comedy

Sam Campbell: Kid Giblet

Kid Giblet is touring the UK through autumn — 29 dates of the reliably unhinged, Taskmaster-champion strain of surrealism that either delights you immediately or leaves you gently baffled (there is, reportedly, no third option). He's also headlining the Roundhouse Comedy Festival in Camden, 1–18 August, alongside Fern Brady — two names already on the "rates highly" list, in one building, for anyone who fancies making a trip of it.

Food & drink Chorley area

The Travellers Rest, Chorley

A proper old-world pub with an open fire and, apparently, a small library tucked into one of its cosier corners — which is roughly the exact brief you'd write if asked to design your ideal unpretentious dinner spot. Low lighting, conversation-friendly volume, the kind of place that doesn't require anything more ambitious than showing up hungry. A strong candidate for the next "treat, not takeaway" evening.

Days out Chorley area

Brindle Distillery — gin-making workshop

Home of Cuckoo Gin, and a ten-minute drive from Wheelton, Brindle Distillery runs workshops where you make your own bottle rather than just watching someone else do it — which feels appropriately hands-on for someone who is "always exploring" gin rather than settling on one. Sunday looks clear on the calendar; this would fill it nicely, and you get to take the evidence home with you.

Food & drink Chorley area

Blackedge Brewery, Horwich

Award-winning cask and craft beer out of Horwich, with free delivery straight to Chorley/Bolton/Wigan — which is to say, there is no excuse not to have something decent in for Wednesday night. England v Argentina at Leah and Decs deserves better than whatever's left in the fridge; consider this the semi-final's official sponsor.

Podcast

Latent Space

Hosted by Swyx and Alessio Fanelli, this is the podcast the people actually building AI infrastructure listen to — agentic frameworks, foundation model economics, the plumbing behind the headlines, discussed by people who'd know. A step more technical than The Artificial Intelligence Show or AI for Humans, but exactly the sort of thing to have on for a chore-filled afternoon if you want the version with more depth and fewer hot takes.
