Weekly recommendations · 14 Sep 2026

Back, and straight into it.

No overnight trips this week, which sounds restful until you look closer: Tuesday's a there-and-back day to Birmingham, up before six, and Thursday's the same trick to London and back via Wigan, home gone half eight. That's two genuinely brutal single-day trips either side of an ordinary Wednesday. So this list is built for trains and exhaustion first, and a proper quiet weekend back on home turf second.

Music

Sports Team

Britrock with a wink, new material out this month — big choruses, minimal pretension, built for exactly the kind of six-a.m. platform standing you've got coming on Thursday.

Reading

Crimson in Quietus — Eugen Bacon

Cyberpunk with teeth: an assassin sent after "lifers," the ultra-wealthy who've bought their way out of dying. Dark, inventive, and short enough to actually finish across two long train days rather than abandoning at chapter three.

Screen

Below (Netflix)

Josh Hartnett as a Newfoundland fisherman whose father died decades ago under mysterious circumstances, now facing something unexplained out at sea. Moody, cold, atmospheric — six episodes, which is about the right size for a genuinely tired week.

Comedy

Stavros Halkias: Uncle Stav

New Netflix special, out this week — no ticket, no venue, no getting dressed. Given the week you've got, "funny thing that comes to you" beats "funny thing you travel to" on every count.

Podcast

Money Trauma

A newer podcast digging into modern anxieties about wealth — thoughtful rather than preachy, and oddly well-suited to someone who's a saver at heart but keeps having to talk himself into spending on the good stuff. Good long-train-journey thinking material.

Food & drink Chorley area

Barolo Bar & Restaurant, Chorley

Cosy, unhurried, properly Italian — the sort of place that suits collapsing into a chair after two exhausting commutes rather than one requiring conversation-worthy effort. Good candidate for Friday night.

Days out Chorley area

Anglezarke Reservoir loop

Waterside and woodland, farmland in between, nothing steep or demanding — precisely the register you'll want after a week of platforms and delay notifications. Morris will approve.

Days out Chorley area

Cuerden Valley Park

More nature reserve than park, dog-friendly, with a decent café at the end of it — a gentler alternative to Anglezarke if the weekend calls for something even more low-key. Either works; take whichever one the weather allows.
