Weekly recommendations · 17 Aug 2026

London, then a barn in Cumbria.

The busiest week in a while. Six-a-side Monday night, a London day trip Wednesday for Arti's leaving lunch (train there and back, work squeezed either side), sushi at Leah and Dec's Thursday once the holiday cash is sorted, and then Friday afternoon it's pets to the kennels and cattery and off to Cumbria for Hannah and Sean's wedding — a whole weekend at Hidden River Barn, right on the Scottish border. Mostly background listening and reading for the gaps this week, plus a couple of things for the two places you'll actually be standing still.

Music

Citizen — "Halcyon Blues"

Raw emo and post-hardcore pulled together into something more cohesive than either label suggests — the kind of record that sits comfortably next to Fall Out Boy and Sleep Token without copying either. Good six-a-side warm-up listening, frankly.

Reading

Time Travel for Beginners — Jaclyn Moriarty

Three people on a collision course through time, using the mechanics of time travel to poke at regret, identity and the small decisions that turn out to matter most — standalone, a bit of "otherness" without needing a magic system to deliver it. Decent company for two long train rides to London and back.

Screen

Lanterns

HBO's new Green Lantern series, playing more cosmic detective noir than superhero spectacle, with Aaron Pierre and Kyle Chandler carrying most of the weight. A different register to the usual list, but the sort of thing that rewards a proper sit-down rather than half-attention — save it for after the wedding, not during wedding-prep week.

Comedy

Nish Kumar — "Angry Humour From A Really Nice Guy"

New UK tour, starting this September, built around turning forty and being annoyed about it — sharp, topical, more clever-punning than absurdist, which makes a change of pace from the last few weeks without straying far from what actually lands for you.

Podcast

Hard Fork

The New York Times' tech podcast, hosted by Kevin Roose and Casey Newton — broader and more accessible than the usual AI-specific rotation, genuinely entertaining rather than just informative. Good company on the Wednesday train down, no spreadsheet required.

Food & drink City of London

One Under Lime Coffee House

Tucked beneath the Lloyd's building on Lime Street, next to Leadenhall Market — proper coffee and an inventive breakfast-through-after-work menu, a five-minute walk from the Willis Building. Worth getting there early Wednesday before the day gets away from you.

Food & drink Chorley area

Elliot's Bistro, Chorley

Traditional cooking, warm hospitality, local produce prepared on-site daily — the sort of unfussy place that suits an evening squeezed between two much bigger commitments, if Monday or Tuesday leave any room for one.

Days out Longtown area, Cumbria

Lanercost Priory

A short drive from Hidden River Barn — medieval priory ruins on the banks of the River Irthing, right in Hadrian's Wall country. Not a full day out, just somewhere quiet and properly atmospheric for a Sunday morning before the drive home, if the wedding's left you wanting fresh air and fewer people rather than more.
