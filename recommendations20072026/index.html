Weekly recommendations · 20 Jul 2026

A full week, then Texas.

More going on than last week — football Monday, tea at your mum and dad's Tuesday, a day in Manchester and a catch-up with John Chrimes Thursday — before Saturday morning hands you a flight to Dallas. So this week's list splits in two: a few things for the evenings you're already busy, and a couple for when you land somewhere hot, new, and entirely lacking in a Brindle Distillery.

Music

Wunderhorse

Jacob Slater's Cornwall-formed four-piece, somewhere between raw and anthemic — the kind of guitar record that rewards being played loud in the car rather than politely through headphones. If Sam Fender's directness and The 1975's ear for a chorus are both doing something for you, this sits right in that gap. Good driving-to-Manchester music, frankly.

Reading · Audiobook

The Magic of Recluce — L. E. Modesitt Jr.

Book one of a genre stalwart built almost entirely around its magic system — order and chaos as opposing forces, with the consequences of every choice worked through properly rather than hand-waved. It's the sort of measured, rules-first fantasy that Sanderson fans tend to fall for once they find it, and at a shade over 19 hours it's practically engineered for a Manchester–Dallas flight. Reread-on-a-plane, reread-to-sleep — this one does both jobs.

Screen

Two Weeks in August

BBC ensemble drama from the Sally4Ever creator — old friends, a Mediterranean holiday, and the sort of secrets that were always going to surface once everyone's had a couple of drinks. Witty and sharp on friendship and betrayal, with a proper dark comedic edge running underneath — the same trick Slow Horses and Ted Lasso pull in their own ways, just relocated to a sun lounger.

Comedy

Ed Gamble — Roundhouse Comedy Festival

Already a name on the list, and he's on at the Roundhouse Comedy Festival in Camden this August — worth flagging now while the dates are still fresh, especially if a trip down is on the cards around the same time as Sam Campbell's slot there. Two birds, etc.

Food & drink Chorley area

Amigos Mexican & Steak House, Chorley

A proper Mexican fix without leaving Lancashire — timely, given a Tex-Mex trip is coming at the weekend anyway, so this is either good preparation or a fair comparison point once you're back. Fresh over Tex-Mex-heavy, which suits the stated cuisine preference rather better than most of the alternatives nearby.

Days out Dallas, Texas

Dallas World Aquarium

A three-level indoor rainforest exhibit, penguins, giant river otters, and a shark tunnel — all thoroughly air-conditioned, which matters more than usual given a heat ceiling of roughly 23°C and a Texan July that will not be respecting it. Animal content plus a guaranteed escape from the heat is about as close to a sure thing as this list gets.

Food & drink Dallas, Texas

El Fenix, Dallas

The place that more or less invented Tex-Mex as Dallas knows it — cheese enchiladas under chili gravy, done the way they've been doing it for decades. Pescatarian rules suspended for the trip, as agreed, so this is the one to go in on properly rather than picking around the edges.

Podcast

TWIML AI Podcast

Sam Charrington's long-running interview show, pulling in researchers and practitioners from across ML and AI for conversations with a bit more room to breathe than the news-cycle shows. A different register to Latent Space — less infrastructure, more "here's what this person actually thinks" — and decent company for the airport wait on Saturday morning.
