Weekly recommendations · 27 Jul 2026

Still in Dallas.

Calendar's suspiciously empty this week — no WFH blocks, no meetings, just Grandad's birthday on Sunday and a flight home not booked until the 5th. Which means you're in Texas for the whole thing, so this week leans hard into Dallas itself, with a few things to have on in the background wherever you end up sitting still.

Music

Whitelands — "Sunlight Echoes"

Leading a small shoegaze revival, pairing dense, wall-of-sound guitars with genuinely vivid melodies underneath — more hook than haze, despite the genre's reputation. Good poolside-or-hotel-room listening: enough texture to reward headphones, enough tune to hum along without really trying.

Reading

Isles of the Emberdark — Brandon Sanderson

A new standalone set within the Cosmere — so all the scale, magic-system rigour and occasional humour you already know you like, without needing to wait for six more books first. About as close to a guaranteed hit as this list gets.

Screen

Margo's Got Money Troubles

Apple TV's dramedy about a college dropout turned unlikely OnlyFans creator with a new baby and mounting bills — sounds like a hard sell on paper, plays as warm, funny and unexpectedly precise about the stakes underneath the jokes. 96% on Rotten Tomatoes and already renewed, so it's not just critics being kind.

Podcast

No Priors

Elad Gil and Sarah Guo talking to the people actually building frontier AI companies — less plumbing than Latent Space, more "where does this industry actually go," which makes it decent thinking material for a long-haul flight home in a week and a half.

Food & drink Dallas, Texas

Malai Kitchen, Uptown

Thai and Vietnamese, made fresh daily, from a husband-and-wife team who built the menu around their own travels through both countries — drunken noodles and basil fried rice get the most love, and the crusted trout keeps things pescatarian-friendly if you fancy it, holiday exemption notwithstanding. Uptown, so easy to fold into an evening out.

Food & drink Dallas, Texas

The Exchange, AT&T Discovery District

A downtown food hall with enough stalls and two full bars that nobody has to agree on one cuisine — useful on a trip where decisions have presumably been made all week. Sit outside in the Discovery District's plaza afterwards if the heat's dropped enough to make that bearable.

Days out Dallas, Texas

Deep Ellum

The quirky end of Dallas — street art, independent shops, and a record shop (Off the Record) that also happens to serve craft beer on tap, which is basically a venue designed around two separate entries in your own tastes at once. Round it off with a slice from Emporium Pies, a local favourite. Best explored on foot, early evening once the sun's lost some of its opinions.

Days out Dallas, Texas

A night at Dallas Comedy Club

Stand-up, improv and storytelling most nights of the week, small-room enough to suit your usual preference over arena gigs. Worth a look at whatever's on while you're there rather than chasing a specific name — the small-medium-venue format alone is most of the appeal.
