Weekly recommendations · 28 Sep 2026

A quiet one, mostly.

Genuinely light. Thursday's marked for October garden tasks — foxgloves in, bulbs layered, garlic and onions into the empty bed — and there's also a standing reminder that the Alba White Truffle Festival starts that day, which, since nobody's booked a flight to Piedmont, we'll treat as a nudge rather than an itinerary. Saturday's the only real outing: uni friends in Manchester, evening. Everything else is built around the garden and a properly quiet week at home.

Music

Anyhow

Confident, guitar-forward indie rock with a debut album on the way — "Harder Than Before / Can't Move" is the one to start with. Solid gardening-playlist material, assuming the foxgloves don't mind a bit of noise.

Reading

The Fires of December — Brandon Sanderson

A new standalone set within the Cosmere: a plague spreading, and only one person left who can warn the king's court in time. Same rigour and scale as everything else of his you've read, no series debt attached — a good one for a quiet week with actual reading time in it.

Screen

Brothers (Apple TV)

Matthew McConaughey and Woody Harrelson playing fictionalised, considerably more unhinged versions of themselves — action and drama with a genuine streak of lunacy running through it. Lighter than most of what's made this list lately, deliberately.

Comedy

Lucy Beaumont — surreal stand-up tour

Touring since September, diving into her own television exploits and general chaotic existence with the kind of surreal streak that's usually a safe bet round here. Worth checking dates near you.

Podcast

Football Ramble

Back on the list after a while away — the season's well underway now, so worth rejoining for whatever chaos Bolton's Championship return has produced since you last checked in.

Food & drink Manchester city centre

Sister Moon, Treehouse Hotel

Southeast Asian barbecue, fourteen floors up, from a chef with proper pedigree in the city's Thai food scene — about as close to a direct hit on your favourite cuisine as Manchester gets. A good venue for catching up with the uni crowd Saturday, view included.

Days out Chorley area

Birkacre Garden Centre

Sat right inside Yarrow Valley Country Park, which means the October task list — foxglove plugs, tulip and daffodil bulbs, hardneck garlic, overwintering onions — can all be sourced in one trip, with a walk built in either side if the weather holds.

Food & drink Chorley area

HOME, Buckshaw Village

Local produce, a menu that actually moves with the seasons — worth asking what's landed on the specials board this week, autumn being exactly the season worth asking about. A good quiet-week dinner, no occasion required.
